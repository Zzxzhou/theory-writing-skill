# Style Exemplars

External authors are evidence about reader experience, not templates for sentence-level imitation. Borrow organization, proof visibility, and explanatory rhythm. Do not copy distinctive wording, notation, or paper-specific conventions.

## Dennis Shen

Primary entry point: [Dennis Shen's publications](https://deshen24.github.io/publications/).

Useful examples:

- [Benign overfitting beyond prediction: Towards a characterization of the feature space](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.15769)
- [On Model Identification and Out-of-Sample Prediction of Principal Component Regression](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume26/23-0102/23-0102.pdf)
- [Causal Matrix Completion](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.15154.pdf)

Borrow these structural habits:

- Give a long proof a compact roadmap whose steps name actual mathematical operations.
- Start from an explicit identity or estimand and keep the derivation visible.
- Explain why cross terms vanish or why an equality follows from a stated assumption.
- Separate logically dependent modules so a reader knows the input and output of each step.
- Use short signposts around calculations and keep the prose tied to the mathematical task.
- Organize introductory prose around questions, obstacles, and corresponding contributions.

Do not universalize every feature of an exemplar:

- Appendix placement remains conditional on the current document mode.
- Preserve the current project's notation and theorem organization.
- Do not add headings to a short proof that reads better as one continuous argument.
- Do not imitate author-specific phrases or reproduce passages.

## Anti-Defensive Writing

Reference: [Anti-Defensive Writing](https://github.com/Kiterlin/anti-defensive-writing/tree/main).

Borrow the preference for direct claims, positive framing, and removing apology-like or referee-facing prose. Modify it for theory research:

- Keep caveats that are assumptions, quantifiers, boundaries, counterexamples, or distinctions between mathematical objects.
- Remove only qualifications that do not change the logical content.
- Replace vague hedging with the exact source of uncertainty or scope.
- Never trade mathematical honesty for confidence of tone.

The resulting voice should be calm, exact, and reader-oriented: confident about what is proved and explicit about what is not.
